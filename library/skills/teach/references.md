# Teach: the research behind each step

Each row names a step in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md), the finding it rests on, and where to read it. Summaries are in our
own words. Check the original before quoting numbers.

| Step in the skill | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Hand it over, quizzes | **Testing effect.** Recalling material on a test improves later retention more than restudying it, even though restudying makes people feel more confident. | Roediger and Karpicke (2006), "Test-Enhanced Learning", *Psychological Science* 17, 249-255. [PDF](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf) |
| Quizzes, flashcards, study guides, review schedule | **Practice testing and spaced (distributed) practice** got the highest utility rating of ten techniques reviewed. Rereading, highlighting and summarizing were among those rated low. | Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1). [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html), DOI 10.1177/1529100612453266 |
| Mix topics and problem types | **Interleaving.** Mixing problem types during practice beat grouping them by type on a delayed math test in classroom studies. | Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig and Cheung (2020), "A randomized controlled trial of interleaved mathematics practice", *Journal of Educational Psychology* 112, 40-52. [IES project summary](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/efficacy-study-interleaved-mathematics-practice) |
| Show it worked, teach one small piece | **Worked examples and cognitive load.** Early in learning a skill, studying worked examples is more effective than solving problems; later, solving problems is better. Fading worked steps bridges the two. | Renkl and Atkinson (2003), "Structuring the transition from example study to problem solving". [ASU record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/structuring-the-transition-from-example-study-to-problem-solving--2) |
| Adapt: less help for those who know the basics | **Expertise reversal effect.** Support that helps novices can become redundant and even harmful for more experienced learners. | Kalyuga (2007), "Expertise reversal effect and its implications for learner-tailored instruction", *Educational Psychology Review* 19, 509-539. [DOI](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-007-9054-3) |
| Explain it back in their own words | **Self-explanation.** Learners who explain worked examples to themselves learn more, and prompting learners to explain improves understanding. | Chi et al. (1989), "Self-explanations: How students study and use examples in learning to solve problems". [PDF](https://pcl.sitehost.iu.edu/rgoldsto/courses/cogscilearning/chiselfexplanation.pdf); [ASU overview](https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/self-explanation_effect.pdf) |
| Find the starting point, respond to their answer | **Formative assessment.** Checking understanding during learning and acting on it raises achievement. | Black and Wiliam (1998), "Inside the Black Box". [PDF](https://www.michigan.gov/documents/mde/Inside_The_Black_Box_-_Black_and_William_601104_7.pdf) |
| Hint ladder, fading support | **Scaffolding and the zone of proximal development.** A tutor supports what the learner can't yet do alone, and hands control back as skill grows. | Wood, Bruner and Ross (1976), "The Role of Tutoring in Problem Solving", *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry* 17(2), 89-100. [History of the metaphor](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/the-early-history-of-the-scaffolding-metaphor-bernstein-luria-vyg/) |
| Close only when they can show it | **Mastery learning and tutoring.** Tutoring combined with mastery learning (move on only after mastery) produced large gains over classroom teaching. Later reviews find smaller but still real gains for step-by-step tutoring. | Bloom (1984), "The 2 Sigma Problem", *Educational Researcher*. [Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_2_sigma_problem); VanLehn (2011), *Educational Psychologist* 46(4), 197-221. [Slides](https://quality.mit.edu/files/2012/01/Quality-Symposium-Kurt-VanLehn.pdf) |
| Graded work | **Academic integrity.** Defined by six values: honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility and courage. | International Center for Academic Integrity. [Fundamental values](https://www.academicintegrity.org/fundamental) |

## Notes on the evidence
- Bloom's two standard deviations rests on a small number of studies. VanLehn's review found a mean effect of about
  0.79 for human tutoring. Treat "tutoring helps" as solid and the exact size as uncertain.
- Dunlosky et al. rated self-explanation and interleaving as moderate, mostly because the evidence was more limited
  at the time, not because they failed.
